Known problems by model year

Before buying any of these, enter the VIN at NHTSA recall lookup by VIN ↗ to see open recalls, and ask the dealer for proof that they've been done.

2026 · new generation (hybrid)

First model year of a new design: four software and module recalls so far

So far there have been recalls for the brake control module (loss of anti-lock brakes and stability control, NHTSA 26V223), the trailer brake module (26V059), the rearview camera not showing (26V531), and tire pressure warnings not working (26V559). All are fixed by a dealer software update or a part swap. It's normal for a first-year model to pick up recalls like this, but there's no long-term reliability record yet. Ask the dealer to confirm every recall is done before delivery.

Source: 2026 Cherokee recall list ↗

2014 – 2015

Transmission problems: the years to avoid

The 9-speed automatic shifts roughly, hesitates and hunts for gears, and some owners report stalling. Chrysler issued recalls and software updates, but problems persisted for some owners. These two years account for most of this generation's complaints.

Sources: Samarins: 2014-2022 Cherokee problems ↗ · JeepProblems: complaints by year ↗

2014 – 2019 · 2.4L 4-cylinder engine

Oil use (the "Tigershark" engine)

Many 2.4L engines burn oil between changes, and low oil can cause stalling. A class-action settlement covers 2014-2019 Cherokees built before July 2018 with a 7-year/100,000-mile extended warranty from the in-service date. That has run out for most of the cars listed here. On any 2014-2019 Sport, Latitude or Limited, ask which engine it has, check the oil level, and ask for oil-change records.

Sources: CarComplaints: Tigershark settlement ↗ · Settlement site (Wood v. FCA) ↗

2014 – 2022 · 3.2L V6 engine

Rocker-arm ticking and oil-cooler leaks

A ticking sound from the top of the engine usually means worn rocker arms, and left alone it can damage the camshaft. The oil cooler and filter housing also tend to leak. Listen for ticking on a cold start and look for oil around the filter housing.

Source: Samarins: 2014-2022 Cherokee problems ↗

All years · 4x4 models

Power transfer unit (PTU) failures

The part that sends power to the rear wheels fails often enough to have had recalls (W47, W48). Replacing one costs about $1,600. Listen for whining or clunking, and check for leaks near the front of the transmission.

Source: Samarins: 2014-2022 Cherokee problems ↗

2016 – 2022

Steadily better

Complaints drop sharply after 2015. 2016 rates below average, while 2017 and 2019-2021 rate average. The 2019 refresh added an optional 2.0L turbo engine, and no major pattern of problems has turned up with it. 2020-2022 have the fewest reported complaints.

Sources: Samarins: 2014-2022 Cherokee problems ↗ · JeepProblems: complaints by year ↗

Known problems by model year

Before buying, enter the VIN at NHTSA recall lookup by VIN ↗ to see open recalls, and ask the dealer for proof that they've been done.

2026 · current model

Several software recalls this year

Recalls that apply to gas Limiteds cover delayed airbag deployment (26V328), windshield wipers that can stop working (26V529) and the rearview camera not showing (26V531). All three are fixed by a software update. Two more recalls, for battery fire risk and loss of drive power, apply only to the 4xe plug-in hybrid, and none of the cars listed here is a 4xe. Ask the dealer to confirm every recall is done before delivery.

Sources: 2026 Grand Cherokee recall list ↗ · NHTSA: 2026 Grand Cherokee recalls ↗

2022 – 2023 · first years of the current design

Suspension recalls and rough shifting

About 330,000 trucks were recalled because rear coil springs could come loose (23V-413; air-suspension models aren't affected). Another recall covers upper control arm bolts that could snap (24V-132), and another the rearview camera (24V-436). A 2023 service bulletin fixes jerky shifting with a software update. Check that all of these were done.

Source: PickupTruckTalk: 2022-2026 known problems and recalls ↗

2022 – 2025 · 4xe plug-in hybrid only

Shutdowns and battery fire risk

Recalls cover the vehicle shutting down while driving (23V-412) and a battery defect that can cause fires (24V-720; owners were told to park outside until it was fixed). Jeep dropped the 4xe after 2025. None of the listings here is a 4xe, but steer clear of a used one unless the battery work is documented.

Source: PickupTruckTalk: 2022-2026 known problems and recalls ↗

2014 – 2021 · 3.6L V6 (previous design)

Oil filter housing and cooler leaks

The plastic oil filter housing and cooler on the 3.6L V6 commonly crack and leak oil. It's a known repair and not a deal-breaker, but look for oil on top of the engine and ask whether the housing has been replaced.

Source: Ecogard: Pentastar oil filter housing leaks ↗

2014 – 2015

The years with the most complaints

These years have the most complaints by far, about transmission problems, electrical faults and stalling. The confusing gear shifter led to a rollaway recall (16V-240), which added an automatic shift to Park.

Sources: CarComplaintData: Grand Cherokee by year ↗ · NHTSA recall 16V-240 report ↗

2019 – 2020 and 2023 – 2025

The better years

Complaints are low for 2019-2020 at the end of the previous design, and again for 2024-2025 once the current design settled in. 2018 and 2021 rate average.

Source: CarComplaintData: Grand Cherokee by year ↗
